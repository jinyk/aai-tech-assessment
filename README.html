Coding Assessment: Paper Chat

Applied AI team, Hudson Bay Capital. Take about 10 minutes to read this page and look through the code before you start.

The project

starter/ is a small chat bot that answers questions about five research papers on AI agents. A script reads the PDFs, cuts the text into short passages and stores them in a search index. When you ask a question, an agent searches the index and writes an answer from the passages it gets back. You can also upload more PDFs from the chat page.

The session

  • One hour, in our office, on a laptop we provide, with the project set up. We give you an OpenAI key; it is already in starter/.env. The Claude Code and Codex command-line tools, VS Code and Cursor are installed. Sign in with your own Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor account, and use a coding agent the way you normally work.
  • You share the screen on a Zoom call. We record it so a second interviewer can review it.
  • Think out loud and commit as you go. In the last 5 minutes, tell us what you found, what you changed, how you measured it, and what you would do next.
  • We do not expect you to finish. How you find the problem and what you choose to do matters more than how many questions you fix.

Task 1: the answers are not good

People who tried the bot say its answers are often wrong, incomplete or made up. Here are six questions they asked. The bot gets them wrong most of the time. Its answers change from run to run. The answers below are real answers from the bot as you get it.

#QuestionThe bot todayA good answer
1What share of ReAct's failed HotpotQA cases were search result errors? About 56% of ReAct’s failed HotpotQA cases were search result errors. 23%.react.pdf, page 6
2In the generative agents interview evaluation, which conditions were compared? They compared the full generative agent, three ablations removing memory types (no observation, no reflection, no planning), and a human crowdworker-authored condition. Five: the full agent; no observation, reflection or planning; no reflection or planning; no reflection; and human crowdworkers.generative-agents.pdf, page 13
3Which model powers Toolformer's question answering tool, and what was it fine-tuned on? Toolformer’s question-answering tool is a Wikipedia search tool, and it was fine-tuned on GPT-J. Atlas, fine-tuned on Natural Questions.toolformer.pdf, page 4
4ReAct and Reflexion both test on ALFWorld. What success does each paper report for its own method? On ALFWorld, ReAct reports about 34% success for its own method, while Reflexion reports 71% success for its own method. ReAct: 71% for its best trial. Reflexion: 130 of 134 tasks.react.pdf, page 8; reflexion.pdf, page 5
5Which paper builds a self-consistency baseline by sampling 21 chain-of-thought trajectories? It’s Wang et al., 2022a/b: “Self-consistency improves chain of thought reasoning in language models.” The ReAct paper.react.pdf, page 5

Find out why, and make the answers better. You decide what to change. You do not need to fix all six. Show us how you know the answers got better, not only that they look better.

Keep the same five papers and the same chat model. Free, open-source Python packages are fine; paid services are not.
